## 13. Confirmed defect

### GRID-CROSS-001 — wrong margin/liquidation contract

- **Severity:** critical
- **Type:** CONFIRMED DEFECT
- **Primary files:** `app/recommender.py`, `app/main.py`, `app/ui/static/app.js`
- **Primary functions:** `_mode`, `_max_liquidation_safe_grid_leverage`, `_build_trade_plan`, `_validate_trade_plan_against_bybit_meta`, `_operator_decision_context_for_reco`

#### Input/path

A normal Bybit Linear USDT Futures Grid recommendation was generated with `margin_mode=isolated`. Leverage safety was derived from a generic isolated-position liquidation-price approximation, and strict preflight rejected `cross` while requiring `isolated`.

#### Actual behavior in v1.0.34

- generated payload: `account_mode=unified`, `margin_mode=isolated`;
- trade plan did not explicitly publish the real cross/one-way product contract;
- strict preflight rejected canonical `margin_mode=cross`;
- operator UI showed an estimated isolated liquidation price;
- leverage selection used a single-position isolated approximation rather than grid commitment and bot equity at the kill switches.

#### Expected behavior

- generated payload uses unified/cross/one-way;
- isolated payload is blocked;
- safety gate evaluates whether dedicated bot capital survives an adverse monotonic move to either external kill-switch after initial margin, inventory loss, execution cost and maintenance reserve;
- no exact or standalone isolated liquidation price is claimed;
- private wallet equity, other positions/orders, risk tier, mark price and the live liquidation engine remain external executor checks.

#### Violated invariant

The recommendation and preflight contracts did not match the exchange product being modelled. Consequently the displayed liquidation buffer was not a valid safety oracle for a Bybit Futures Grid Bot.

#### Financial/trading impact

The old formula could approve or reject leverage on a non-equivalent risk model and present a false numerical sense of distance from liquidation. Direction and exact magnitude of error depend on account equity, other positions/orders, risk tier and mark price; therefore historical isolated-buffer numbers cannot be treated as evidence of safety or profitability.

#### Why existing tests missed it

The suite was internally consistent but encoded the stale `isolated` assumption in many fixtures and assertions. Green tests proved conformance to that internal contract, not conformance to current exchange mechanics.

#### Reproduction

`python -m pytest -q tests/test_iteration223_bybit_cross_margin_contract.py`

Pristine result: **8 failed in 1.22s**.

Key RED evidence:

- generated `isolated` instead of `cross`;
- trade plan lacked `margin_mode`;
- cross preflight returned `MARGIN_MODE_UNSUPPORTED`;
- no cross-margin stress helper existed;
- generated economics published a numeric isolated liquidation price;
- frontend/docs lacked the cross-margin contract.

#### Correction

- added `arithmetic_grid_cross_margin_stress` in `app/grid_math.py`;
- generated plans now publish unified/cross/one-way;
- leverage selection evaluates both kill-switch sides through the shared stress helper;
- strict preflight recomputes the stress from canonical plan geometry and does not trust a supplied buffer;
- isolated mode is blocked fail-closed;
- standalone isolated liquidation price is not published for Futures Grid;
- frontend and operator artifacts show cross-margin equity buffer and the external-account limitation.

#### GREEN evidence

The same test passes twice:

- `8 passed in 0.91s`
- `8 passed in 0.89s`

#### Residual risk

The deterministic stress is intentionally conservative but is not Bybit's private liquidation engine. It does not know live wallet equity, cross-collateral interactions, other positions/orders, changing risk tiers, mark-price jumps or the actual execution/slippage path. The external executor/reconciler must recheck those values immediately before launch.
